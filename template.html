Who gets to own Quebec infrastructure.

Quebec is building the largest energy expansion in its history and its mid-market is changing hands. Most of the record sits in French — Régie de l'énergie dockets, Hydro-Québec tender results, provincial decrees — and is read by almost no one on Bay Street or in New York.

This tracker reads those filings and publishes them as structured English. Every figure carries its source and a confidence flag R-4227-2023. Where a number is sealed, it says so instead of estimating.

Method

What gets recorded

Sponsor and sponsor type, asset and asset type, capacity, province, stage, offtake counterparty and tenor, rating, acquirer origin and capital source. Each field carries its own source and date, not one citation for the whole row.

The disclosure cliff

Privatization is itself a disclosure event. When a Quebec issuer goes private, its filings stop and its press archive is often pruned — Innergex now retains five press items and no investor section. Boralex will follow at close. This tracker records the last public state of an asset before the record closes, because after that no amount of searching recovers it.

What does not

Per-developer PPA pricing, debt tranche margins, mezzanine terms and mid-market multiples are sealed or redacted in Quebec filings. They are marked confidential rather than estimated. A tracker that guesses at them is worth less than one that admits the boundary.

Confidence flags

Disclosed stated by a party or a regulator. Reported from credible secondary press, not yet confirmed in a filing. Confidential exists but is sealed. Unverified carried from secondary reporting and not yet confirmed in a primary document — shown so the gap is visible rather than hidden.
